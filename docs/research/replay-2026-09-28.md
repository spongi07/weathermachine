# Replay of 28 September 2026 — why nothing traded, and where the edge was

*Question (28 September 2026).* After the complete evaluation log
(11:58–14:28) and a closer look at the current bot: is the proposal still the
same? Replay the day step by step and find out how an edge can be created.

**Short answer.**

* **The core of the proposal stands: the model is the bottleneck.** The
  replay adds a third structural fault to the two found earlier. A report
  that repeats the high restarts the model's clock (and the confirmation
  gate), so a plateau at 21 °C looked like a brand-new high.
* **On this day the only money was in the first half hour after 21 °C was
  reached** (11:58–12:28): 21 °C YES at 0.82–0.86 and 22 °C NO at
  0.79–0.89. From 12:58 every price was 0.90 or more for an outcome the
  market already knew, and from 13:28 it was 0.996–0.997: above the 0.99
  cap, and a loss after fee and slippage even without the cap.
* **To trade there, the model had to be at least as sure as the market,
  within minutes of the first 21 °C.** Buying 21 °C at 0.82 needed a model
  probability of about 0.88 that 21 °C was final; the model said 0.05.
  Nothing in the rules can fix that; only better information can.
* **What changed in the proposal.**
  * The clock fix is new.
  * "Re-test 30′ against 60′" became a measurement with money: every
    confirmation window, ask range and model structure is now replayed at
    the prices the market actually traded, on every settled day.
  * One day is not evidence, so nothing reaches trading without passing a
    test that was fixed before seeing any result.

## 3. What the model got wrong

1. **The whole morning is one cell.** `h<12` pools new highs from midnight
   to noon, mostly early-morning highs that the day then far exceeds. At
   11:58, three minutes after 21 °C, the model gave **54 % to 24 °C or
   more**; the market gave that about 0.3 %.
2. **A repeated high restarts the clock.** At 12:25 the METAR repeated 21 °C.
   The model's clock and the confirmation gate both count from the *last*
   report at the high, so 21 °C, held for 30 minutes, was treated as brand
   new (m000-029). The 60′ gate opened only at 13:28, when the price was
   already 0.997. The same happened on 27 September, when the high was
   retested every half hour and the clock never started.
3. **Thin cells make the probability run backwards.** From 13:28 to 13:57
   nothing happened except 30 more minutes at 20 °C. Yet P(21 °C final)
   *fell* from 0.61 to 0.55, and P(22 °C) was still 0.38 at 13:57. The cell
   "a high set before 12:30 that has held 90 minutes, 1 °C below it, in
   autumn, at 12–13 h" is rare on a clock that restarts at every repeat. Its
   probabilities are mostly borrowed from broader cells about other
   situations.
4. **The forecast input compares the forecast with itself.** The rise is
   the forecast's rest-of-day maximum minus its own maximum so far. It
   cannot see that the *observed* 21 °C at 11:55 had already reached the
   forecast's 21.4 °C day maximum. The market could, and priced 21 °C at
   about 0.80 within minutes.
5. **Weather the model does not see.** By 13:55 the METAR carried
   `TEMPO RA`, from 14:25 light rain: no more warming. The model reads only
   temperatures, clocks and (adopted) forecast inputs; the market also reads
   radar and the sky.

## 4. Would a rule change have made money on this day?

| change | effect on 28 September |
|---|---|
| 30′ confirmation instead of 60′ | 12:58: ask in range, but the model said 0.50–0.58 against a price ≥ 0.90. No trade. |
| 0′ (no confirmation) | 11:58/12:28: the model said 0.05/0.27 against 0.82/0.86. No A trade. |
| wider ask range (0.70–0.99) | Same model numbers: no A trade. B 22 °C NO at 0.79 (11:58) was the one near-miss (below). |
| no market pooling | B 22 °C NO at 11:58: model 0.818 vs ask 0.79 gives EV +0.015 ≥ 0.01. Pooled with the market's 0.765 it became 0.793 and EV −0.011. It would have won (+$2.49 on $10), but only with 0′, the wider range *and* no pooling together. |
| no 0.99 cap | 13:28–14:28 at 0.996–0.997: after fee and slippage every share loses money, even when right. |
| faster data for D | Every dead bucket was already at 0.999 or had no seller. Nothing to take. |

Loosening the rules alone would not have made money. The one near-miss needed
three rules removed at once. One day cannot justify that, and at 0.79–0.99 a
single loss erases 4–100 wins.

## 5. Where the edge was — and what it takes

| trade | would have made (on $10) | needed P(win) after pooling | needed model P | model had |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 21 °C YES at 0.82, 11:58 | +$2.04 | 0.842 | ≈ 0.88 (market ≈ 0.80) | 0.053 |
| 21 °C YES at 0.86, 12:28 | +$1.50 | 0.881 | ≈ 0.91 (market ≈ 0.84) | 0.273 |
| 22 °C NO at 0.79, 11:58 | +$2.49 | 0.813 | 0.854 (market 0.765, logged) | 0.818 |

"Needed" = break-even (ask + fee + slippage) plus the 0.01 minimum edge. The
live rule pools logit p = ½·logit(model) + ½·logit(market) and caps the
result at the model, so the model must be as far above the needed value as
the market is below it. The YES midpoints were not logged; they are taken as
the ask minus 0.02, the gap the logged 22 °C NO market showed.

The edge on this day was **being as sure as the market, early** — not being
faster, and not accepting worse prices later. It required knowing, at 11:58,
that:

* the observed high had reached the forecast maximum (headroom ≈ 0);
* a high first reached at 11:55 on a late-September day has little
  warming left;
* weather was coming in.

The first two are what the candidate structure adds; the third is the next
lever (§9).

## 6. Is the proposal still the same?

| earlier proposal | after the full log | status |
|---|---|---|
| Finer late-morning hours | Kept: the morning is split into < 09, 09, 10 and 11 h | built |
| Forecast headroom (rest-of-day forecast maximum minus observed high) | Kept, as the candidate's forecast input, tested with its own placebo | built |
| — | **New:** the clock counts from the *first* report at the high, so repeats no longer restart it | built |
| Same walk-forward test as the forecast | Kept: prequential, both structures in one pass, fixed rule (below) | built |
| Re-test 30′ against 60′ | **Changed:** measured in money at traded prices, 0′/30′/60′ × two ask ranges × both structures, on every settled day | built |
| Check against the market with `research market` | Kept, plus a report-by-report replay of chosen days (`--day`) | built |

## 7. What this change builds

* **Candidate structure** (`ModelStructure::Candidate`, pre-registered):
  * levels `[MinutesSinceFirstHigh] → [+Drop] → [+Season] → [+LocalHourFine]`;
  * forecast refinement **ForecastHeadroom**, with buckets
    below (≤ −1.0 °C), level (−0.9…+0.4), above1 (+0.5…+1.4) and above2.
* **Selection at training** (`wm_backtest::selection`):
  * Both structures are trained in the same prequential pass. Each
    structure's forecast input goes through its own placebo-controlled
    evaluation.
  * Every report from 10:00 to 18:00 after a 365-day burn-in is predicted
    by both, as the service would use them.
  * The candidate replaces the current structure only with ≥ 365 scored
    days and a 95 % day-block interval of the log-loss change entirely
    below zero.
  * The verdict is stored in the model (`selection`). It appears in the
    training report ("Model structure — walk-forward comparison"), in the
    log and in the dashboard's MODEL tooltip.
  * A model trained without the comparison is retrained once after
    deployment, in the background, while the current model keeps trading.
* **`research market`:**
  * both structures are scored against the market;
  * "Strategies at traded prices" replays A and B for every variant, with
    trades, wins, P&L per trade with a day-block interval, and totals at
    the live stake;
  * `--day YYYY-MM-DD` adds a report-by-report replay showing both models'
    cells and probabilities, the market, the ask and every simulated trade.
* **Dashboard:** the forecast box shows the headroom next to the rise.

## 8. How to check it on the host

1. **Deploy the new image.** The model retrains once in the background
   (about as long as a normal training). Afterwards the MODEL pill's tooltip
   and `/data/research/eham-survival.md` state whether the candidate was
   adopted, with the log-loss change by hour and for repeated highs.
2. **Replay this day and the season, with money.** In Portainer, run a
   one-off container on the stack's data volume (see
   [edge-research.md §4](edge-research.md)):

   ```sh
   research market --from 2026-06-01 --to 2026-09-28 --day 2026-09-28 --day 2026-09-27 --print
   ```

   The day replay shows, report by report, what each structure believed
   and at which price. The strategy table shows what each variant would
   have earned.
3. **Decide by a rule fixed now.**
   * A structure change is live only if training adopts it.
   * A rule change (window, ask range) is live only if its variant shows a
     positive P&L whose 95 % interval lies above zero on settled days
     *after* the variant was chosen. Twenty-four variants are replayed, so
     the best one on past days is expected to look better than it is.
   * If the market predicts better than both structures, A and B should
     stay quiet; not trading is then the right result.

## 9. Next levers (not built)

* **Weather in the model.** Present weather and cloud from the METAR itself
  (RA/SHRA/TEMPO, cloud cover, wind shift) as a refinement, evaluated like
  the forecast. The data is already in every report we store.
* **A parametric model.** An ordinal regression on continuous features
  (clocks, drop, hour, headroom, weather) instead of cells. It would use
  more information without thinning cells (fault 3).
* **KNMI 10-minute observations**
  ([dataset](https://english.knmidata.nl/open-data/10-minute-in-situ-meteorological-observations),
  [notification service](https://developer.dataplatform.knmi.nl/notification-service)).
  These show the trend between METARs and the first touch of a new high
  sooner. They are only worth building if the replay shows the model close
  to the market but late.
* **Providing liquidity.** Quoting instead of taking earns the spread and
  rebates. It needs order management (Phase 14).
